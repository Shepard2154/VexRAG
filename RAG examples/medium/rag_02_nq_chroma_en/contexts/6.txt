The speed of light in vacuum, commonly denoted c, is a universal physical constant that is exactly equal to 299792458 meters per second by definition of the meter. According to special relativity, c is the maximum speed at which all conventional matter and hence all known forms of information in the universe can travel. It is the speed at which massless particles and changes of the associated fields travel in vacuum. Such particles and waves travel at c regardless of the motion of the source or the inertial reference frame of the observer.
